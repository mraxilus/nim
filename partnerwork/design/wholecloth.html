Whole cloth

Everything agreed so far, redrawn from the ground up. Six ideas, in the order each one needs the last: the marks, the heights, the holds, the modifiers each arm can wear, the turning that writes them, and the moves between states. The accepted conventions are kept exactly — the chevron-circle body, the two arm inks with a deep shade for the lead, square against round, and the level fills. plate 1 · the marks

The conventions, kept exactly

These are settled, so everything after this plate is drawn in them and nothing restates them. A dancer is a circle with a chevron for the facing. A hand is its arm's ink — blue for left, orange for right — worn deep by the lead and plain by the follow; the lead's hands are squares, the follow's are rounds, so ink says which arm, shade and shape say whose. The fill on a hand is a height, defined on the next plate.

a dancer — the chevron is the facing; the rim is the body the arms go round left arms — blue; the lead's deep and square, the follow's plain and round. A mark is an arm's anchor, fixed at the body's side; it never moves right arms — orange, the same way round a connection — two arms meeting end to end. The rope of a mark's colour is that arm, and its end is the hand; the seam where the two ropes meet is the joined hands, the deep rope always the lead's. Straight wherever it can be; it leaves a mark at its edge so the fill stays readable faded — a free hand: nobody holds it, but it keeps its identity. A free arm is never drawn; only a held arm has a rope

plate 2 · the heights

A level is a height, and nothing else

Your definitions place the three levels on the body itself: low is below the shoulder, about the torso; high is above it, about the neck; above clears the head. A level says where a connection rides — it says nothing about which arm passes over which; that is said by the drawing itself, on plate 4. Because the pages are drawn from overhead, height is carried by the fill.

Above is different in kind, and the drawing says so: a hand above sits over the head — inside the circle — on the very axis the couple turns about. That placement is why plate 5 will find it turning without limit, and why it can never wrap or lock.

plate 3 · the holds

Six holds, each at its own rest

A hold names which hands are joined — nothing more. It is written lead to follow, case carrying who: Left to left is the lead's Left holding the follow's left. Every hold is drawn at its rest — the orientation where nothing is wound. For the cross-name holds that is face to face; for the same-name holds it is the follow turned away, where the connection runs clean down one side. At rest, every connection is a straight line.

Why the follow-away rest matters. Drawn this way, Left to left at rest is the same shape as Left to right at rest — only her facing, and so which of her hands, differs. The two two-hand holds become one geometry offset by her facing: a half turn puts either pair in the X, with her away for the cross-name pair and facing for the same-name one. Neither X is a hold of its own. Counted from face to face the away rest is @ −½; the body sim reads her arm there as open — the hand at her side — and as the low lock, led, only at the whole turn.

The over/under in the X is the drawing's say. The body sim finds the two arms of the X at the torso going round opposite sides of her, one across her front and one behind her back, so there is no crossing in plan for it to settle; above the head it counts the crossings and names which is over. The crossing break — the broken line passes under — is used between two connections only; two arms of one body say their over/under by track and fill, on the next plate.

plate 4 · the modifiers

What one arm can do, at its height

A modifier belongs to one arm of one dancer — either dancer — and it rides inside that hand's own name: Left-Lock-Low to right is the lead's Left, locked low, holding the follow's right. Two drawing rules carry the anatomy. An arm goes around the outside of the body circle, leaving its mark at the angle that lets it hug the rim — only an above hand may sit inside, and never over the chevron. And only a held arm is drawn: under or over her other arm is said by the height and by how close the rope hugs the body — low and tight is under, high and wide is over — never by drawing the free arm. The mark never moves: what moves is the rope, and the hand is where the rope ends — a low lock ends just behind the opposite shoulder, a wrap at the opposite front, and a high lock comes back to its own shoulder: a small loopback at the mark, on the facing side — the elbow bends forward, never behind — the way a state loops to itself on an automaton. A connection is only ever drawn whole, hand to hand: an arm is never shown alone. Each figure was asked of the rope model; the turns count from face to face.

What carries each fact: the track says lock or wrap (round the back, or across the front); where the rope ends says which lock (behind the opposite shoulder low; the loopback, forward, to its own shoulder high); the fill on the fixed mark says the height, and with it under or over — the track's width only repeats that. Nothing is written twice, so nothing can disagree. The turns in the captions count from face to face.

plate 5 · turning

Turning is what writes the modifiers

A position is a hold turned some number of quarters, counted from face to face as the sheet counts; the same-name holds are drawn at their away rest, which sits a half turn along. And orientation is not decoration: turn the held dancer and the connection winds across her body, which is where wraps and locks come from. Your one worked row says it for Left to left held low: half a turn one way is the wrap; a whole turn the other way is the lock.

plate 6 · the moves

Two primitives, four named compounds

Everything that changes what is held reduces to two primitives; the four starred helpers are worth their names because a lead thinks of each as one action. The starred ones keep contact through a trace — a hand travelling on the partner, not through the air. Definitions only for now: the symbols for these are deliberately unchosen, and the next round will offer candidates.

collect primitive

Join a connection: a lead hand takes a follow hand.

drop primitive

Release it: the connection ends, both hands go free.

flick compound *

A drop with momentum: the hand is sent, not just let go.

trace compound *

Slide a held connection along the partner's body: contact, moving.

place compound *

Collect, then drop somewhere new — traced the whole way, with nothing in the path.

cut compound *

Drop, then collect past an obstruction — an arm, torso, or neck in the way; the trace carries the contact over it.

Where the safety edge lives: among these moves. Any move that would produce a …-Lock-Low hand must arrive from a low carriage — collect low and turn, or trace down first. The state itself is fine; the forbidden thing is one particular way in.
