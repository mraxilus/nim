The state space

Every frame two facing bodies can hold, hand to hand

A frame is what each of the lead's hands holds, plus which arm lies on top where the forearms overlap. Two laws close it: a hand of the follow is held by at most one hand of the lead, and an arm order is recorded exactly where the forearms overlap. That is the whole state space.

{{gallery}}
lead's Left arm lead's Right arm Seen from above · lead on the left, follow on the right · the dashed line is the midline between the bodies

Crossing is derived, not listed. Facing partners are in mirror, so the lead's Left faces the follow's right. A connection is parallel when it joins opposite-named hands and crossed when it joins same-named hands. Two connections overlap only when both cross — which happens for exactly one pair of holds. That is why Left-to-left and Right-to-right has an over/under distinction and Left-to-right and Right-to-left does not, and your sheet already has it exactly that way. Nothing has to remember the rule; it falls out of who faces whom.

2. closed and half-closed are not hand positions

{{deferred}} cells depend on it

These two are the only states in your sheet that are not hand-to-hand, and the cells reaching them say what they are:

half-closed —slide→ Right to left
closed —slide→ Left-to-right and Right-to-left

A slide keeps contact, and contact can only travel along a body — there is no path from one of the follow's hands to the other. So in both cells the lead's right hand must start on the follow's body and slide down the arm it is already touching to reach their left hand. That is the classic closed hold: left hand to the follow's right, right hand on their back.

Reading them the way vocabulary does instead — half-closed = Left to right, closed = Left-to-left and Right-to-right — makes them duplicates of rows you already have, and contradicts six of your own cells.

Do: hold them out until the body vocabulary lands with rotation. They are not lost, they are queued — along with trace, which needs the same thing.

The three questions in the margins

Unsure if it is correct to either separate or combine these positions? I'm leaning heavily towards them being the same thing. Perhaps I need a clearer definition of what a frame position is.

One position, two states. Keep both readings.

A frame position is which hands hold what — Left-to-left and Right-to-right. It is what you call out on the floor. A frame is a position plus the arm order. It is what the state machine moves between.

The test is whether a dancer can pass between them for free. They cannot: going from over-Left to over-Right takes a cut. Merge them and the machine offers a move the arms forbid; name them separately everywhere and you report a new position every time an arm changes height. In the code this costs one function — describe gives the state, position gives the class.

Rotation

What follows for free, and what needs measuring

The twelve turn sheets are empty, so this is a proposal rather than a reading. The quantity a turn adds is twist: how far the follow's body has turned relative to the lead's. It is one number for the couple, not one per arm, because both bodies are rigid and both arms see the same relative rotation.

Two things that need no measurement at all

A rotation of the whole couple stores no twist. It is why you can travel round the floor without unwinding — and why a model that added up each dancer's turns separately would be wrong.

The parity of the twist re-reads the whole base matrix. At half a turn the follow's back is to the lead, their left hand is now on the lead's left, and every connection that was crossed is parallel. The frames do not change; the way they are read does. That is one function, not a second matrix.

Three numbers that do

From the one datum available — “if you're hand to hand you can only do 1 full rotation comfortably”:

What joins the bodiesCapacityWhy
one hand-to-hand connection1 turn both dancers share the twist across two arms
two hand-to-hand connections½ turn the arms form a loop and bind
a hand on the bodynone the arm is already around the partner

Three numbers, and they already predict three things that match the floor: a wrap is led from a two-hand hold and a lock from a one-hand hold; a turn out of closed needs the back hand to leave first; and a full turn on two hands is not comfortable.

The four cells worth dancing

Your rotations sheet has two filled cells, both for Left to left held low: half a turn left gives a wrap, a full turn right gives a lock. Two different rules fit both cells — magnitude only (one half-turn wraps, two lock) and direction decides (one way carries the arm across the front and wraps, the other behind the back and locks, at either size). They disagree about exactly four cells nobody has filled in:

All four are Left to left, held low. Dance them, write down what the arm does, and the twelve turn sheets stop being twelve matrices to fill in and become one function of three numbers. That is worth more than the other 760 empty cells put together.

What is running

The model, the validator, and the laws

One state, one relation

A move exists between two frames exactly when the difference between them is one primitive. The names, the routes, the app and the audit are all derived from that — there is no table of moves to maintain.

A validator, not a toy

The browser app shows the frame you are in, every frame one move away, and every frame that is not — with how far away it is. Only what is offered can be clicked, so a move the ontology does not derive cannot be danced.
