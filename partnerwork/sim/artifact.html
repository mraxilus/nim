The body sim

Two bodies of the average adult's measurements — a torso of its tape round, a neck and a head stacked on it — and arms of three links on a shoulder that swings and twists, an elbow that bends and a wrist that bends any way, each joint with the range a dancer tolerates. Join hands, turn a body, and the arms take the most comfortable pose that holds — where the solver puts them, not where a drawing would like them. Keep turning and a joint runs out, and the page says which. Nothing here stands for anything; it is the model, drawn.

Below the plan is the same pose from the side, which is where an elbow's height and which of two crossing arms is over can be seen. A drag of a turn slider carries the arms on by small moves, so an arm that has gone round a body stays round it, and where no small move holds the slider stops: it snaps back to the turn the arms reached and the page says what blocked them. Join a hand, move a height or the rig, and the pose is settled afresh. The sweep button turns the follow from face to face until a joint runs out, and is the authority for where a turn blocks: a drag never takes the fresh pose a sweep may.
